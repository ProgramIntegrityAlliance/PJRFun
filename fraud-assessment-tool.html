Welcome to the Fraud Prevention Capability Assessment

This assessment will help you evaluate your organization's fraud prevention maturity across five critical domains:

  • Governance and Accountability: Leadership, policies, and roles
  • Fraud Risk Assessment: How you identify and prioritize fraud risks
  • Data and Analytics: Using data to prevent and detect fraud
  • Controls and Operations: Prevention and detection mechanisms
  • Monitoring and Learning: Performance tracking and continuous improvement

The assessment takes approximately 10-15 minutes to complete. You'll answer 15 questions using a 5-point scale, and receive immediate feedback with actionable insights.

Your responses are saved automatically in your browser, so you can return to complete it later if needed.

Governance and accountability
Consider policies, roles, and leadership expectations for fraud prevention.
1. Clear roles and responsibilities for fraud risk management are defined and communicated.
2. Senior leaders receive regular reporting on fraud risks and prevention activity.
3. There is a documented fraud risk management policy that covers prevention, detection, and response.
Fraud risk assessment
Think about how you identify, assess, and prioritise fraud risks.
4. The organisation regularly conducts structured fraud risk assessments for key programmes or processes.
5. Fraud risk assessments consider inherent and residual risk and link to control design.
6. Fraud risks are integrated into broader enterprise risk management and planning.
Data and analytics for fraud prevention
Focus on how you use data to prevent and detect fraud, not only to investigate losses.
7. Key datasets (for example, payments, eligibility, identity, vendor data) are available and linked for fraud analysis.
8. The organisation uses analytics or rules to flag high-risk cases before payment or decision.
9. Data engineers or analysts work with fraud and programme staff to refine models or rules over time.
Controls and operations
Think about controls in design and in practice, including automation and manual checks.
10. Key fraud controls are documented, mapped to risks, and applied consistently across regions or units.
11. Preventive controls are embedded in core systems and processes, not limited to detective or after-the-fact checks.
12. There are clear procedures for handling suspected fraud cases, including escalation and suspension of payments where appropriate.
Monitoring, oversight, and learning
Look at how you track performance, respond to findings, and improve over time.
13. The organisation tracks indicators for fraud prevention performance, not only fraud outcomes or losses.
14. Oversight body recommendations related to fraud are tracked, prioritised, and implemented with clear owners.
15. Lessons from detected fraud cases inform design changes in policy, process, or controls.
